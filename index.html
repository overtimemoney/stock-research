The 5% Quiz

How much more do you think you'd make if you earned an extra 5% per year?

Meet the average 401(k) saver. They invest about $500 a month — roughly 9.6% of a typical paycheck, the national average contribution rate (Fidelity). At a typical 10% per year, after 40 years they'd have

$2.66 million

They only put in $240,000. Compounding did the rest.

Years of investing $500/month

Same $500 a month, same 40 years — but 15% per year instead of 10%. Where do they end up?

4× as much money. Same savings, same 40 years — just 5% more per year = $8.02 million more.

And at 20% per year — 10% more than the 401(k)? Same $500 a month.

16.6× the 401(k). $44.06M vs $2.66M — from the same $500 a month.

20% a year, every year, is something almost nobody can do — that's Warren Buffett territory. But 15%? That's just the difference between knowing what to buy… and what not to buy.

This app helps you tell the difference.

Hypothetical example for education only — not financial advice, and not a prediction.
